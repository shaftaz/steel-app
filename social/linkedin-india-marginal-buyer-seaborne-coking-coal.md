# LinkedIn draft — India as coking coal's marginal buyer (india-marginal-buyer-seaborne-coking-coal-2026)

39.94 Mt of coking coal through India's major ports in seven months, up 10.85%. Crude steel output in the same months: up 2.6%.

That gap is the story. India isn't just making more steel; it's making steel in a more coal-intensive way, through the only large blast-furnace fleet in the world that is still being built.

Meanwhile the buyer that set seaborne coking coal prices for twenty years is leaving the water. China took over half its met coal imports from Mongolia by rail last year. Japan and Korea are closing furnaces. The IEA attributes all of 2026's seaborne growth to India and Indonesia.

So the marginal tonne of seaborne coking coal is becoming an Indian tonne. The buyer with the least flexibility (high-ash domestic coal, nothing to substitute at the top of the cycle) is becoming the buyer that clears the market.

The 2030 arithmetic: India needs ~50 Mt more a year. That's 16% of the entire seaborne market, while BMA guides flat and Anglo has sold out of Queensland.
